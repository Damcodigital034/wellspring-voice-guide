The part you usually have to explain

Some of the hardest things to talk about are the ones you would normally have to explain first

Why moving out can be complicated. Why you feel responsible for your parents. Why family opinions carry so much weight. Why conversations about relationships feel loaded. Why "just set a boundary" was never that simple.

Our families are often built on deep love, sacrifice, and connection. Those same strengths are part of what holds us together, and they can also make certain conversations, boundaries, and life transitions feel more complicated.

Here, you don't have to translate any of that. Working with a therapist from your own community means the context is already understood, so you can spend your time on what actually matters to you.

The expectations

The respectable career, the right timeline, and the questions that come up at every family gathering.

Being the strong one

Holding it together for everyone, with little room to admit you're running on empty.

Family and relationships

Boundaries, romantic relationships, and feeling responsible for your parents' wellbeing.

Carrying it quietly

The stress and burnout you've learned to push through without telling anyone.

When something feels off

It doesn't always show up as sadness

Struggling doesn't always look the way people expect. It can show up as slipping grades or focus, pulling away from people you love, feeling tired no matter how much you rest, getting irritable over small things, or aches and headaches with no clear cause.

And having a family that loves you doesn't mean you can't be struggling. Support at home is a real strength, but it doesn't make what you're feeling any less real, or mean you have to carry it on your own.

You don't have to choose

Therapy here is not about choosing yourself over your family, or your independence over your culture

There is no ranking between what you need and what your family and community value. You can keep your individuality and stay connected to the people and culture that matter to you.

Therapy isn't about choosing yourself over your family. It's about understanding yourself more clearly, navigating difficult decisions with more confidence, strengthening the relationships that matter, and working through whatever feels important to you right now.

You come away with more clarity, steadier footing in your own life, and more of yourself to bring to the people you love, not less.

About diagnosis, honestly

Therapy here isn't about labeling you

We're not here to decide something is wrong with you. Therapy is a space to understand yourself and what matters to you, while navigating whatever life is asking of you right now.

If a diagnosis ever does come up, often because insurance needs one to cover your care, we talk it through with you openly. Nothing here is hidden or decided behind your back.

Common questions

A few things people often ask

Is what I share kept private?

Yes. Your conversations are confidential. Therapy is your space, and what you bring stays between you and your therapist.

What if I'm nervous about being given a diagnosis?

That's a common worry. We don't lead with labels. If a diagnosis ever comes up, often because insurance requires one to cover care, we talk it through with you openly first.

Do you take my insurance?

We accept most commercial plans, including BCBS, Aetna, UnitedHealthcare, Priority Health, and McLaren. Reach out and we'll confirm your specific coverage.

Can I be seen online?

Yes. We offer online therapy across Michigan, as well as in-person sessions in Troy and Clinton Township. Evenings and weekends are available.

How soon can I be seen?

Most clients are seen within 48 hours. There's no waitlist.
